Write a resume that gets read

Recruiters scan a resume in seconds. Lead with a two-line summary of what you do and the level you work at, then list roles newest first.

  • Start each bullet with an action and end with a result: "Cut month-end close from 10 days to 6 by automating reconciliations."
  • Use numbers wherever you honestly can: money, time, volume, people.
  • Keep it to one or two pages, in a simple layout that applicant-tracking systems can read.
  • In Canada, leave out your photo, date of birth and marital status.

Tailor every application

A resume that mirrors the job posting's language does better with both people and screening software.

  • Reorder your bullets so the experience the posting asks for comes first.
  • Use the posting's own terms for skills you genuinely have.
  • Never claim experience you don't have; interviews find it out.

Search smarter

  • Search a few related titles: "Data Analyst", "Business Intelligence Analyst", "Reporting Analyst".
  • Check companies' own career sites; many roles never reach the big job boards.
  • Apply within the first few days a job is posted, when recruiters are reviewing most actively.
  • Set up alerts so new matches come to you.

Prepare for interviews

  • Prepare five short stories using Situation, Task, Action, Result; most behavioural questions fit one of them.
  • Research the company's products, customers and recent news.
  • Prepare two or three questions about the team and how success is measured.
  • Send a brief thank-you note within a day.

Evaluate and negotiate offers

  • Compare total compensation: base, bonus, equity, benefits, RRSP matching and vacation.
  • Research the market range for the role and city before you discuss numbers.
  • Ask for changes politely and in one conversation, with a specific number and a reason.
  • Get the final offer in writing before you resign from your current job.
